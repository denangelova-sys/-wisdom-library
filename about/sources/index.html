Sources & Methodology

Wisdom Library is a curated reference project. It distinguishes a verbatim quotation from an editorial rendering of a source passage.

Quotation labels

Verified quotation Exact wording checked against the cited edition or public-domain translation.

Faithful rendering A concise English rendering based on the cited passage. It is not presented as verbatim wording.

Attribution uncertain Used only when provenance is genuinely unclear; such entries are candidates for removal or further research.

Primary-source principle

Classical entries are checked against identifiable editions where possible. Project Gutenberg is used as a convenient public-domain repository for several classical translations. The source title, passage and translator are recorded when available.

Why wording varies

Ancient works exist in multiple translations. A phrase can therefore be historically authentic while its English wording differs between editions. Wisdom Library does not merge different translations into a synthetic quotation.

Editorial standard

Popular internet quotations are not automatically treated as authentic. If a famous line cannot be traced to a reliable source, it should remain a rendering, be attributed to the actual modern writer, or be removed.
