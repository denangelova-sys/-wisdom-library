Sources & Methodology

Wisdom Library separates source-backed material into two labels: Verified quotation for wording checked against a cited edition, and Faithful rendering for an editorial English rendering based on a cited passage.

Why this distinction matters

Classical works often exist in multiple translations. A translation can be faithful to the same passage without matching another translator word for word. We therefore do not present editorial renderings as verbatim quotations.

Primary source library

Where possible, the collection links directly to public-domain editions such as Project Gutenberg. For example, Project Gutenberg hosts Marcus Aurelius' Meditations and Epictetus' Enchiridion.

Editorial and AI-assisted process

Wisdom Library uses AI-assisted drafting and technical generation for parts of the site, but source labels are not treated as proof of verbatim quotation. Where a page is marked Verified quotation, the wording is tied to the cited edition. Where it is marked Faithful rendering, the text is intentionally presented as an editorial rendering rather than a published translation.

Readers should use the linked source for the primary text and consult the cited edition when exact wording matters.
